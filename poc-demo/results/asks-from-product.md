# What we need from product (David), 2026-09-10

Ordered by how much of the design depends on the answer. Each line: the question, why it is open, our recommended answer.

1. **Failure behavior wording and the retry window.** Requirements: at-least-once, retry then abandon with the cause logged, "worth discussing" for unreachable destinations. Design: "drop on failure". Pool: configurable deadline then a counted drop (30 s default in the POC; today's real behavior is a silent stall). Needed: confirm "retry for a configurable window, then abandon and count" is the contract, and the default window (we propose 5 minutes, matching what customers implicitly get today, with the drop visible). Also whether "wait instead of drop" (the deferred-delivery buffer) is v1 or a later flag.
2. **Validation.** Requirements: destination tested automatically on create and update, no user-facing test API. Design: test off by default, opt-in, plus a test action. Needed: a decision. Our recommendation: mandatory test on create and update as required, plus a skip flag for infrastructure-as-code (create the destination before the broker exists), and no separate test API.
3. **Detach semantics.** After the upgrade, removing a destination from a bucket no longer drops events already queued for it; delete is the immediate stop. Today those queued events are dropped silently. Needed: acknowledgement that detach is not a revocation, a release-note line, and whether a pause or disable flag on the destination is wanted at GA.
4. **Ordering.** The requirements do not mention it; customers rebuild object state from event order. Needed: per-object ordering written in as the target, and a decision on whether the known rebalance exception (measured) is a GA gate or a documented limitation.
5. **Destination naming across kinds.** Every legacy component matches a destination by name only; an account-scoped destination named like a global one is delivered to the global one today (measured). Needed: may an account-scoped name equal a global name (then the matcher must compare full ARNs everywhere and legacy components get a patch), or do account-scoped names live in their own namespace? Also confirm that global destinations stay usable by every account, as the requirements say, and that this is an accepted isolation gap for global ones.
6. **Overlapping rules.** Today an event that matches rules for two destinations goes to both, and we keep that. AWS rejects overlapping filters for the same event type at PUT time. Needed: keep accepting overlaps (our recommendation, nothing changes for existing customers) or reject them on new configurations for AWS parity.
7. **Kerberos scope.** Requirements: not required for account-scoped destinations in v1, required for global. The per-connection producer now works, so account-scoped Kerberos is cheap. Needed: keep it descoped or include it. And acknowledgement that global Kerberos depends on fixing the shipped image first (BB-828), since the requirement assumes it works today.
8. **Duplicates during operations.** Requirements accept duplicates. Measured: a workgroup change duplicates 18 to 30 records per 100 while the old generation drains, more if a member is stalled; a worker crash re-delivers up to about five seconds of traffic. Needed: confirm these windows are acceptable, and whether `s3.object.sequencer` should be populated at GA so consumers can de-duplicate (it is null today).
9. **Per-account quota.** Requirements: out of scope. We want an ops-configurable cap as a support lever against one tenant exhausting the system-wide 10,000. Needed: confirm the cap is acceptable as an operator setting, not a customer feature.
10. **Upgrade expectations.** One-way switch, applied by Ansible as a container swap (processors stopped, workers started, offsets seeded); no return to per-destination processors. During the switch, delivery pauses for the restart plus the group join (45 to 60 s measured); a bounded window may be delivered twice. Needed: confirm the pause and the duplicate window are acceptable, and that "no rollback to processors" is acceptable.
11. **Numbers we do not have.** Expected event rate per destination and per platform, expected number of distinct Kerberos principals among global destinations, expected destinations per account. Everything in sizing is illustrative until product gives one datapoint.
12. **Monitoring content.** Requirements ask for KPIs, alerts and L1 to L3 dashboards. Needed: the KPI thresholds that count as degradation (backlog age, drop rate, delivery latency) and what the L1 lifeline should show.
13. **SSRF limit on test-notification endpoints.** Requirements moved it to future evolutions; the design has dial-time validation against the deployment's own addresses. Needed: ship it in v1 anyway (our recommendation, it is cheap) or hold it.
14. **Account-scoped Kerberos.** Kerberos is global-only in v1 (pinned, one process per principal, no new dependency). Offering it to accounts later means either operator-provisioned pinned workgroups per principal, or a client that holds several identities in one process: today that is KafkaJS (unmaintained since February 2023), a spike on the maintained pure-JavaScript `@platformatic/kafka` with our GSSAPI mechanism, or a librdkafka patch we would carry. Needed: is account-scoped Kerberos ever a requirement, and if so, is a per-principal process the acceptable shape?
